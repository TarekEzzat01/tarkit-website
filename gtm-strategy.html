What belongs in a go-to-market strategy?

Tarkit · Updated 1 October 2026

A go-to-market strategy defines who you serve, the problem you solve, why buyers should choose you, and how you reach and convert them. It turns commercial choices into a coordinated plan.

In this guideStart with a market choiceDefine the ideal customer and buying teamMake the value proposition specificChoose the motion and the measuresLeave with an operating plan

Start with a market choice

Segment the market around meaningful differences: business needs, buying situations, constraints, and ability to act. Prioritize a segment you can reach and serve; “every company” does not help a team decide.

Define the ideal customer and buying team

An ideal customer profile describes the organizations most likely to benefit. A buying-team map describes the people who influence approval, use, budget, and risk. Keep the two connected, but distinct.

Make the value proposition specific

Explain the customer problem, the change your offer makes possible, and the evidence you can show. Separate verified proof from hypotheses that still need testing.

Choose the motion and the measures

Agree channels, qualification rules, handoffs, and responsibilities. Define how the team will review conversion, cycle time, pipeline quality, and loss reasons. A measure is useful when it changes a decision.

Leave with an operating plan

A practical GTM plan includes a priority segment, customer profile, buyer map, positioning, messaging, channel choices, and an owned experiment backlog. Review it as you learn from real conversations.

Bring it into your next review

Write down the decision you need to make, the evidence you have, and who owns the next action. That is a useful place to start.
