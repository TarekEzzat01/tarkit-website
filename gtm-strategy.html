A practical B2B go-to-market plan

By Tarkit · Published 1 October 2026

Choose your market, define the buying team, and connect positioning to a sales motion. A worked B2B example and a reusable GTM worksheet.

Start with the decisions, not the channels

A go-to-market strategy sets out which buyers you will serve, the problem you solve, why they should choose you, and how the team will reach and convert them. A campaign is one way to execute that strategy; it cannot settle an unclear market choice.

For a B2B team selling in Saudi Arabia, the wider Gulf, or Egypt, start with one buying situation. Record the market, offer, customer problem, and evidence separately. Do not assume the same buyer roles, procurement steps, language preferences, or value argument apply to every account in a region.

The output should let another seller make a useful decision without needing the founder to reinterpret it each time.

A worked market choice

Illustrative example — not a client engagement. Imagine a software provider that already sells in Egypt and wants to test demand from facilities-service companies in Saudi Arabia. Its product helps teams coordinate maintenance work. It has no verified Saudi results, so market fit remains a hypothesis.

Illustrative GTM decision map

Priority accountFacilities-service companies coordinating several customer sitesInterviews confirming how work is assigned and reviewed
ProblemManagers lose context between reported issues and assigned workA buyer’s account of the current process and its consequences
TriggerA company is adding sites or changing its service processA directly confirmed operational change
Reason to consider the offerOne shared view of requests, owners, and statusA relevant workflow demonstration with buyer feedback

This map is a starting point for research, not a list of market facts. If buyers do not recognize the problem, revise the segment or proposition before increasing outreach.

Separate the company profile from the buying team

The ideal customer profile describes the organization: what it does, the situation that makes the problem urgent, and whether it can use the offer. The buying-team map describes the people who influence the decision.

In the example, an operations lead might describe the workflow, a finance reviewer might assess the commercial case, and an IT owner might review access and integration. These are possible roles to investigate, not a rule for Saudi organizations. Ask who uses, approves, funds, and reviews the purchase in the actual account.

For each role, record the question they need answered and the evidence that would answer it. A feature list rarely addresses all four roles.

Write a proposition you can demonstrate

Use a simple structure: “For this buyer in this situation, we help make this task possible through this mechanism.” Keep the mechanism specific and the result within what you can support.

Our illustrative provider could say: “Give site managers one place to assign maintenance requests and review their status.” It should not claim a percentage cost saving or faster resolution without measurement. Keep an evidence column beside each message so the salesperson knows what can be shown.

A useful message changes as evidence improves. Document the buyer’s language and objections rather than treating the first draft as permanent.

Turn the choice into an owned sales motion

Choose an initial channel based on where the buyer can be reached appropriately and what your team can support. Set a qualification rule: for example, the buyer has confirmed a coordination problem, identified the process owner, and agreed a next conversation.

Document who researches accounts, who speaks to buyers, which information moves into the CRM, and what must happen before an opportunity advances. Our growth-engine guide shows how to specify that handoff.

Review relevant conversations, qualified opportunities, time in stage, and reasons for disqualification. Compare results with the original hypothesis. Activity volume alone does not tell you whether the chosen segment is useful.

Use the plan in a decision review

Bring one segment hypothesis, one buying-team map, the messages you tested, and the evidence from recent conversations. Decide what to keep, what to revise, and what remains unknown. Assign the next experiment to an owner and define the observation that would change your mind.

This guide describes Tarkit’s working approach, not a regional benchmark or a promise of sales results. For help turning the decisions into operating documents, see GTM strategy and consulting.
