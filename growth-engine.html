Build a B2B sales handoff that works

By Tarkit · Published 1 October 2026

Define the evidence, owner, next action, and review point for a sales handoff. Includes an illustrative workflow and a reusable worksheet.

What makes a growth engine repeatable?

A growth engine is a connected set of workflows for finding relevant buyers, advancing opportunities, and learning from results. Repeatability comes from clear inputs, ownership, handoffs, and review—not from the number of tools connected.

If a founder still explains every lead or rescues each proposal, find the point where information or responsibility disappears. Fix one handoff before redesigning the whole revenue process.

Specify one handoff end to end

Illustrative example — not a customer result. A B2B services business selling in Egypt and Saudi Arabia wants an account researcher to hand a relevant opportunity to a seller. The company names and roles below are fictional.

Example account-to-opportunity handoff
StepInformation requiredOwner and exit condition
Account selectedReason the company fits; source and date of evidenceResearch owner checks fit, rather than only collecting a contact
Buyer conversationConfirmed problem; known buying roles; open questionsSeller records what the buyer actually said
Qualified opportunityAgreed next action; owner; due date; reason to proceedOpportunity owner accepts the handoff or returns it with a reason
ReviewOutcome of the next action; changed assumptionsCommercial lead reviews exceptions and agrees a correction

“Interested” is not an exit criterion. “The buyer has confirmed the problem and agreed a workflow review on a recorded date” is observable. Use a criterion that fits your sales motion rather than copying this example unchanged.

Keep the record useful to the next person

Start with a small set of fields: account, source, fit rationale, confirmed problem, buying-team roles, stage, owner, next action, due date, and unknowns. A field earns its place when someone uses it to make a decision.

For a team spanning markets, keep market and preferred communication language explicit. Ask the buyer rather than inferring language or decision-making behavior from location. Maintain a shared definition of each stage even when the way a conversation happens differs.

Separate evidence from interpretation. A buyer asking for information does not by itself confirm budget, purchase timing, or authority.

Measure quality alongside activity

Pick measures that answer a review question. If handoffs keep coming back, inspect the reason rather than rewarding the number created.

A small operating scorecard
MeasureDefinition to agreeWhat to investigate
Handoff acceptanceAccepted handoffs divided by submitted handoffs in the same review periodMissing context or weak qualification
Stage progressionOpportunities meeting the next stage’s criteriaWhether the criteria reflect actual buyer progress
Time in stageElapsed days under an agreed start and end definitionWaiting for the buyer, internal work, or an outdated stage
Overdue next actionsActions past their due date that remain openOwnership, workload, or an unrealistic commitment

There are no benchmark targets in this example. Establish a baseline from your own workflow and inspect small samples before interpreting percentages. Compare like-for-like cohorts and keep the definitions stable.

Pilot the process before automating it

Use a small agreed set of opportunities to test the handoff. Ask the receiving owner to complete the next action using the recorded information alone. Note the questions they still need to ask.

Revise the fields and criteria, then automate the reliable parts. Reminders may help an owner act; they do not correct an unclear qualification decision. For AI-enabled steps, apply the workflow readiness checklist before connecting customer information to a model.

Leave a process the team can maintain

The handover pack should include a workflow map, stage definitions, a field dictionary, ownership, exceptions, and the review scorecard. Test it with someone who did not design it.

If the team is still unsure which buyers to pursue, revisit the GTM decisions first. If the market choice is clear but the workflow is fragmented, explore growth engine design.
