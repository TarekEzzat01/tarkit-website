What makes a growth engine repeatable?

Tarkit · Updated 1 October 2026

A growth engine is a connected set of workflows that finds relevant buyers, advances qualified opportunities, and learns from results. Repeatability comes from clear inputs, owners, handoffs, and review.

In this guideConnect the customer journeyDefine inputs and exit criteriaGive every handoff an ownerReview quality alongside volumeDocument what you learn

Connect the customer journey

Map the path from account selection and first contact to qualification, proposal, close, and customer success. Identify where context or responsibility is lost.

Define inputs and exit criteria

A stage name is not a process. Write down what must be true before an opportunity moves forward: a confirmed problem, known stakeholders, an agreed next meeting, or a reviewed proposal.

Give every handoff an owner

Record who acts, what information moves with the work, and when the next action is due. Automations should support these rules instead of hiding unclear responsibilities.

Review quality alongside volume

Activity counts show effort. Pair them with qualified conversion, time in stage, loss reasons, and customer feedback to understand whether the motion is working.

Document what you learn

Test one meaningful assumption at a time. Keep the audience, message, channel, and outcome visible. Adopt what has supporting evidence and revisit what does not.

Bring it into your next review

Write down the decision you need to make, the evidence you have, and who owns the next action. That is a useful place to start.
