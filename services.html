GTM strategy & consulting

When every deal still depends on the founder.

You have a B2B offer, but target accounts, qualification, positioning, or the sales motion still change from one conversation to the next. This is also a starting point when entering a new market with an offer that already sells elsewhere.

What you bring

Bring the offer, recent wins and losses, customer conversations, current messaging, and what is known about buying decisions. The founder or commercial lead participates with the person who will own execution.

What we work through

Choose a priority segment, distinguish the ideal company from its buying team, agree qualification criteria, and turn the value proposition into messages a seller can use. For Saudi, Gulf, or Egyptian opportunities, record market-specific assumptions separately and test them with buyers; a translated pitch alone is not a market-entry plan.

What you take forward

A documented GTM plan, an ICP and buying-team map, a positioning and messaging framework, channel priorities, and an experiment backlog with owners. The working documents make decisions, evidence, and unresolved assumptions visible.

Handover and boundaries

We review the plan against actual opportunities and walk the execution owner through its use. Market-entry work covers commercial decisions; company formation, legal advice, and guaranteed market access are outside this service.

Growth engine design

When activity grows but the handoffs break.

Your team is researching accounts, contacting buyers, and updating the pipeline, but qualification, ownership, and follow-up are inconsistent. Start here when the next hire needs a process to inherit instead of more disconnected tools.

What you bring

Bring a sample of opportunities, stage definitions, activity records, existing tools, and the people responsible for selling and customer handover. Use anonymized examples for initial scoping.

What we work through

Map account selection through qualification, proposal, close, and customer success. Define entry and exit criteria, required information, owners, and dated next actions. Agree the measures that distinguish effort from useful progress.

What you take forward

A revenue workflow, CRM stage and field specifications, a handoff checklist, an ownership map, and a review scorecard. Implementation, automation, and tool configuration are included only where they are explicitly agreed in scope.

Handover and boundaries

Test the workflow on a small agreed set of opportunities, review exceptions, and hand over operating notes to a named owner. This is a process-building engagement; outsourced daily prospecting and an ongoing campaign retainer are separate scopes.

AI infrastructure

When a defined workflow could benefit from AI.

You can name the decision or task you want to improve, but data access, integrations, output quality, or human review are not ready. Start with one bounded workflow rather than adding AI across the whole business.

What you bring

Bring the workflow owner, sample inputs, desired output, source systems, and data-access constraints. Keep passwords and live customer data out of the first enquiry. Relevant technical and security owners join before implementation.

What we work through

Map permitted data flows and system boundaries, assess source quality, define representative evaluation examples, and agree where a person must review an output. Model and tool choices follow the task, operating constraints, and acceptance criteria.

What you take forward

A use-case specification, data and integration map, evaluation checklist, and operating guide. Any implemented pilot has a defined scope, human review points, and documented fallback behavior.

Handover and boundaries

Review the pilot against agreed examples before expanding. Handover covers ownership, monitoring, access, and failure handling. Provider charges, production hosting, and ongoing support are agreed explicitly; no regulatory certification or blanket accuracy claim is implied.

How an engagement starts

A defined scope, a visible review point, and a named owner for the work.

1. Understand the constraint

Discuss the buyer, offer, existing process, and the decision that is getting stuck. We identify the people and evidence needed for useful discovery.

2. Agree the work

Write down deliverables, responsibilities, milestones, acceptance criteria, fees, and any implementation dependencies before work begins. Timing follows the agreed scope and access to inputs.

3. Build, review, and transfer

Review working artifacts with your team, test the assumptions that matter, and document what the execution owner needs to maintain the work. Ongoing support is a separate, explicit decision.

A useful fit starts with access.

Bring a B2B offer, access to commercial decision-makers, and someone who can receive and use the work. You can start before every system is in place, but the team must be able to test decisions with real evidence.

If you need guaranteed revenue, a list of introductions, or legal market-entry advice, this scope will not meet that need. We agree what can be delivered and how it will be reviewed.

Where does growth get stuck?

Bring one recent deal, one broken handoff, or one workflow you want to improve. We’ll use it to find the right starting point.
